## 1. The story and the goal

*Whoever drinks from the River Lethe forgets.*

Below the world of the living runs a black river. The dead must cross it to reach the **Far Shore**, where a veiled **Keeper** waits with one cup of river water. Drink it, and the weight of a whole life slips away.

An old ferryman carries the dead across, but only those who were buried with a coin for the fare. The others, the **coinless dead**, wake alone on the **near shore** and wander the bank. Fog fills them, and they begin to forget: first the small things, then the faces, then their own names. A soul that forgets everything simply fades into the mist.

You are the **Ferrymen**: servants of the underworld, neither living nor dead. The coinless dead that the old ferryman will not carry are left to you. You have no boat. You have something stranger. Along the banks of Lethe grows the **Netherbloom**, a crimson flower of long, curling petals, blooming on a bare stalk with no leaves, because *flower and leaf never meet*. Its glow is the only light in the fog that a lost soul can follow. Where you plant Netherblooms, a soul can follow the river toward the Far Shore.

One more thing: a soul's memories are not yours to tell. The Ferrymen may speak with one another on the water, but never of the memories they carry: only of the river, and of which threads of a life they hold in their hands.

### The goal

Netherbloom is a cooperative game for 1–4 players, and a game lasts about 15 turns. One soul starts on the near shore (space 0). All players win or lose together.

**You win** when the soul token reaches the Far Shore (space 12). The soul moves only onto Lit spaces (spaces with a Bloom), so you place Roots ahead of it and turn them into Blooms (section 5). Along the way, some cards can be inscribed in the Book of Remembrance (section 9); the Book does not decide whether you win, but it decides how the story ends.

**You lose** at once if any of these happens:

- **Faded**: the forgotten pile reaches the Lantern's limit (section 10).
- **Shattered**: the soul has 3 Fractures.
- **Deluge**: you must place a Black Water token but none is left in the supply.

Forgetting cuts both ways. Many strong cards send cards to the forgotten pile. With 6 or more cards forgotten (***Light as Ash***), the soul moves faster; with 9 or more (***Adrift***), faster still (section 4). But every forgotten card brings you closer to Faded.

### The Veiled Memories

Talk as much as you like about your plans, the board, the Loom, the Book, and what each of you intends to do next. But the memories stay veiled. Never show your hand, and never say a card's name, its effect, its Resonance, or the memory written on it. You may say which threads you hold ("I have two Obsession and one Oblivion").

The **Echo** still matters: a card played on its thread as your Answer Resonates (section 3). When the game ends, the Veiled Memories are lifted: everyone may reveal their cards and share their memories, and one player reads the Book aloud (section 9).

## 2. Setting up

Follow these steps in every game; each Soul's special setup is listed in the table that follows them. The illustration shows a game set up for the *Child of the Last Flame* as an example, and its numbers match the steps below. Appendix A lists every component.

1.  **The river board.** Join the three panels in order, with Reach I (and the near shore) on the left and Reach III (ending at the Far Shore) on the right. Each panel has square wells along both banks for the bank tiles, plus its Reach's Mist deck well in the strip along the left bank. The river flows unbroken from panel to panel (section 5).
2.  **Bank tiles.** Sort the bank tiles by the Reach number on their fog side: 6, 8, 6 tiles for Reaches I, II, and III. Shuffle each Reach's tiles separately and place them fog side up in the square wells of spaces 2–4, 5–8, and 9–11. Do not look at their other side (section 5, "Bank tiles").
3.  **The first Blooms and the soul.** Space 1 has no bank tiles: place a **Bloom token** (Bloom side up) on each of its banks. Place the soul token on the near shore (space 0).
4.  **The Soul and the Lantern.** Choose a Soul and place its Soul card face up next to the board; its special rule is in effect for the whole game (section 10). Then choose a **Lantern**. The Lantern sets the difficulty: how many Black Water tokens you have, the Faded limit, and how many harsh Mist cards go in each Reach's deck (section 10, "The Lantern"). For your first game, we suggest the Gentle Lantern. Finally, apply the chosen Soul's special setup from the table below.
5.  **The Memory deck.** Shuffle the 32 core Memory cards together with the chosen Soul's 8-card pack, which includes its Key Memory (section 9). For the *Open Crossing*, use the 8 Echo cards instead. Place the deck face down and leave room beside it for a face-up **discard pile**.
6.  **The Mist decks.** Build one deck for each Reach: its 10 basic Mist cards, plus one *Rising Water* and one *Shade Drawn Near*, plus as many random harsh Mist cards as the Lantern calls for (none at Gentle). Shuffle each deck and place it face down, turned sideways, in its Reach's Mist well along the left bank. Leave room beside each well for that Reach's face-up **Mist discard pile**.
7.  **The Loom of the Fates.** Place the Loom board with its three threads (Obsession, Acceptance, and Oblivion), and put the knots next to it. A thread is full at 4 knots with 2–3 players, and at 5 in solo play or with 4 players. Keep the Echo token next to the Loom: it goes onto a thread when the first card is played face up or Burned (section 6).
8.  **The Mantles.** Place the four Mantle cards face up within reach of all players (section 8).
9.  **The Book of Remembrance.** Open the chosen Soul's Book to page I and keep the photo corners handy (section 9). Sort the Soul's 9 **Keepsake cards** by Reach, shuffle each Reach's 3 cards separately, and place them face down beside the Book in three stacks (I, II, and III). Place the Soul's **ending cards** face down beside them, unread. the *Open Crossing* has no Keepsake or ending cards.
10. **The Ledger and the supply.** Place the Ledger board next to the Loom. Forgotten cards go on its numbered track, and Fractures go in its Fracture wells. Place only as many Black Water tokens as the Lantern allows (6, 5, 4, 3 for Gentle, Standard, Hard, Heroic) in its Black Water well, and return the rest to the box (section 7). Place the Root/Bloom tokens, Shade tokens, Light, Fractures, knots, and the Hold Fast, Shield, and Resignation tokens within reach of all players: this is the **supply**. Put the chosen Soul's starting Light in the shared **Light pool** (see the table below).
11. **The Ferrymen.** Deal each player a hand of cards: 4 in solo play, 3 with 2–4 players. Each player takes a Push Through token and places it face up. The first player takes the Oar. In solo play, shuffle the 12 Ghost cards into a face-down Ghost deck next to you (section 11).

## 3. Your turn

> **You lose at once if:**
>
> - **Faded**: the forgotten pile reaches your Lantern's ✕ on the Ledger.
> - **Shattered**: the soul takes its 3rd Fracture (the last Fracture well on the Ledger).
> - **Deluge**: you must place Black Water and the Ledger's Black Water well is empty.
>
> **You win** when the soul reaches the Far Shore (space 12).

Starting with the first player (who holds the Oar), players take turns in clockwise order. On your turn:

1.  **Summon a Mantle** (optional, before your first action; section 8).
2.  **The Answer** (your first action): play a card (face up, face down, or Burn), Recall, or Push Through.
3.  **The Steer** (your second action): play a card or Push Through. You may Burn only once per turn.
4.  **End of turn**: carry out the end-of-turn steps (section 4).

Your hand size is 4 in solo play and 3 with 2–4 players.

### Playing a card

Play a card from your hand in one of three ways:

- **Face up**: resolve the card's effect, add one knot to its thread on the Loom, and move the Echo to that thread. Then put an Obsession or Acceptance card on the discard pile (or inscribe it in the Book, section 9) and an Oblivion card on the forgotten pile.
- **Face down**: place 1 Root (section 5). Ignore the card's printed effect. No knot is added, and the Echo does not move. Then put the card face up on the discard pile.
- **Burn** (at most once per turn): choose another player who also holds a card of the same thread. You each place one card of that thread face down on the table, then reveal both. Choose one of the two cards: resolve its effect **twice**, add one knot to its thread, and move the Echo to that thread. Then forget both cards, and the other player immediately draws 1 card. If no other player holds a card of the same thread, you cannot Burn. *Solo:* Burn one card from your hand: resolve its effect twice, add one knot, and move the Echo; then forget that card and the top card of the deck.

![card](figures/en_card.jpg)

### Recall and Push Through

**Recall** (Answer only): pay **3 Light** and move any card from the forgotten pile to the discard pile, where it can be drawn again later. A forgotten Key Memory can be Recalled.

**Push Through** (**once per game** per player): you may do this only when Shade tokens occupy **both** banks of the next space and that space has no Black Water. Return both Shade tokens to the supply, move the soul onto that space (gain its Light as usual, section 8) and flip your Push Through token face down. The soul then takes **1 Fracture**, plus 1 more for each Push Through token that was already face down, and you forget the top card of the deck. So the first Push Through costs 1 Fracture, the second costs 2, and the third costs 3 (which means Shattered). Then continue your turn as normal.

### The Echo and Resonance

The **Echo** token sits on the thread of the last card played face up or Burned by any player (or by the Ghost). It does not move for a card played face down, a Recall or a Push Through.

If your **Answer** is a card played face up or Burned on the Echo's thread, the card **Resonates**: also resolve its Resonance effect. On some Oblivion cards the Resonance replaces the normal effect ("Instead"). A Resonating card may be inscribed in the Book of Remembrance (section 9).

Your Steer never Resonates. If your Answer is a Recall or a Push Through, you cannot Resonate this turn. For a Burn, the card that Resonates is the one whose effect you double: its effect happens twice, but its **Resonance happens only once** (except on an "Instead" card, whose Resonance replaces its effect and so happens twice).

![echo](figures/en_echo.jpg)

### Forgetting and the two piles

**Forget** a card: put it face up on the next number of the Ledger's forgotten track, overlapping the card before it. The number above the newest card shows how many cards have been forgotten. **Forget the top card of the deck**: put the top card of the Memory deck on the forgotten track without playing it. If a card leaves the forgotten pile (through a Recall or a Chapter Blessing), slide the later cards down to close the gap.

The **discard pile** (next to the deck) and the **forgotten pile** (on the Ledger) are both face up, and anyone may look through them. Keep them apart: when the Memory deck runs out, the discard pile is shuffled back into the deck; the forgotten pile is never shuffled back. If the Memory deck is empty when you need to draw or forget, shuffle the discard pile into a new deck first.

## 4. The end of the turn

Resolve the end-of-turn steps after **every player's turn** (not once per round), in this order:

1.  **Bloom-Bringer.** If the active player holds the Bloom-Bringer Mantle, they may flip one Root token on or ahead of the soul's space to its Bloom side at no cost.
2.  **Soul Step.** Check these situations in order and do the first one that applies:
    1.  **Is the Resignation token next to the soul?** The soul does not move. Forget the top card of the deck (nothing can prevent this), then remove the token. Go to *e*.
    2.  **Is the next space Lit (at least one bank has a Bloom token) and free of Black Water?** Move the soul onto it. Gain 1 Light for each Bloom token on the space the soul left, then return all Root, Bloom, and Shade tokens on that space to the supply. If the soul has entered the first space of a new Reach, remove all knots from the Loom (the Echo stays on its thread). Then check *c*.
    3.  **Extra move.** Depending on how many cards have been forgotten, the soul may move one more space (an extra move also gains Light for the space the soul leaves). Then go to *e*:
        - With 6 to 8 cards forgotten (***Light as Ash***): the soul moves one more space, but only if that space is Lit and free of Black Water.
        - With 9 or more cards forgotten (***Adrift***): the soul **must** move one more space. If that space is Lit and free of Black Water, it simply moves there; if not, it moves there anyway and takes 1 Fracture. Exception: if a player holds an unused Spirit Warden Mantle, turn the Warden sideways instead; the soul does not make the extra move and takes no Fracture. *The Child of the Last Flame* is never Adrift (section 10).
    4.  **The soul cannot move: it Lingers** (it stays where it is). Check these situations in order and do the first one that applies:
        1.  The Hold Fast token is next to the soul: nothing is forgotten.
        2.  A player holds an unused Spirit Warden Mantle: turn it sideways; nothing is forgotten.
        3.  The soul's own space is Lit: nothing is forgotten.
        4.  The Soul is the *Bride Who Stayed Below* and her special rule has not been used this game: move the soul token back one space; nothing is forgotten (section 10).
        5.  None of these: **forget the top card of the deck**.
    5.  **Shade damage.** If either bank of the soul's space has a Shade token (one or both), the soul takes **1 Fracture**, whether it moved or Lingered.
3.  **Mist.** Reveal the top card of the Mist deck for the soul's current Reach and resolve its instructions in order. "Ahead of the soul" is counted from the soul's space: 1 space ahead is the next space. Then put the card face up on that Reach's **Mist discard pile**, beside its Mist well. If the deck is empty, first shuffle only that Reach's 10 basic Mist cards to form a new deck. Appendix C lists every Mist card.
4.  **Cleanup.**
    - For each space with Black Water and a Root or Bloom token on **both** banks: remove the Black Water token and return it to the box.
    - For each Shade token with a Bloom token on the same bank of the space directly before it **and** the space directly after it: return the Shade token to the supply.
5.  **Draw** back up to your hand size.
6.  **Solo: the Ghost** (section 11). Then do Cleanup again.
7.  **Pass the Oar** to the player on your left. In solo play it comes back to you after the Ghost.

![soulstep](figures/en_soulstep.jpg)

### At the start of the next turn

- Remove the Hold Fast token if it is in play.
- If you hold the Spirit Warden, straighten it at the start of your own turn.
- A Shield token that has not canceled a Fracture is removed at the end of its owner's next turn.

### Effects that happen immediately

A thread that becomes full triggers its **Backlash** immediately (section 6). Page rewards and Chapter Blessings are resolved immediately, as soon as the inscription that earns them is made (section 9). A Memory Site gives its keepsake (with the *Open Crossing*, its 2 Light instead) when its bank tile is flipped. The game ends the moment the soul reaches the Far Shore (you win), the forgotten pile reaches the Lantern's limit (Faded), the soul takes its third Fracture (Shattered), or a Black Water token must be placed with none left in the supply (Deluge).

**Canceling a Fracture.** If the Shield token is next to the soul, or the Soul is the *Child of the Last Flame* and her special rule applies (the first Fracture in each Reach), the Fracture the soul would take is canceled: no Fracture is placed, and it does not count as taken.

## 5. The river, Roots and Blooms

The river has 13 spaces: the near shore (0), spaces 1 to 11, and the Far Shore (12). Each space from 1 to 11 has a **left** and a **right bank**: facing downstream toward the Far Shore, the left side of the river is the left bank and the right side is the right bank. Each bank holds at most one token (Root, Bloom, or Shade). "Ahead of the soul" means the spaces with a higher number than the soul's space; "the next space" is the space directly ahead.

The river has three Reaches: **Reach I, the Asphodel Road** (spaces 1–4), **Reach II, the Fields of Mourning** (5–8), and **Reach III, the Banks of Lethe** (9–11). Each Reach has its own Mist deck and its own page in the Book of Remembrance.

### Roots, Blooms and the network

Netherbloom tokens are double-sided: a **Root token** is one showing its Root side; a **Bloom token** is the same token flipped to its Bloom side. A space is **Lit** when at least one of its banks has a Bloom token. In the Soul Step, the soul moves only onto Lit spaces, unless 9 or more cards are forgotten (***Adrift***, section 4, "Extra move").

**Placing a Root.** Put a Root token on an empty bank (one with no token at all) on or ahead of the soul's space. No Root can be placed on a bank that already has a Shade token. The bank must **touch the network**, meaning at least one of these is true:

- it is on the soul's space or the next space, or
- the other bank of the same space already has a Root or Bloom token, or
- the same bank (left or right) of the space directly before or after it has a Root or Bloom token.

**Blooming a Root.** Flip a Root token on or ahead of the soul's space to its Bloom side. **Withering a Bloom.** Flip a Bloom token back to its Root side; if neither bank of that space now has a Bloom token, the space is no longer Lit.

![network](figures/en_network.jpg)

### Bank tiles

When you place a Root on a bank that is still covered by fog, flip its bank tile face up. The tile stays on the bank, and its type decides what happens:

- **Fertile**: place the Root.
- **Stony**: place the Root only if the card's effect says "even on Stony ground" or you hold the Root-Weaver Mantle. Otherwise, no Root is placed; return the Root token to the supply.
- **Memory Site**: place the Root and find a **keepsake**: take the top card of the Keepsake stack for this bank's Reach, read it aloud, and place it face up beside that Reach's page of the Book. Keepsakes have no effect on play; they are read with the Book at the end (section 9). the *Open Crossing* has no Keepsake cards: gain 2 Light instead. With any other Soul, a Memory Site gives no Light.
- **Grave**: no Root is placed. Put a Shade token on this bank.

A bank with no bank tile (both banks of space 1, or a bank whose Grave tile was removed) counts as Fertile. A face-up bank tile follows the same rules: Roots can be placed on Fertile and Memory Site banks, on Stony banks only as described above, and never on a Grave. Reach I has 3 Fertile, 1 Stony, and 2 Memory Site tiles; Reach II has 3 Fertile, 2 Stony, 2 Memory Site, and 1 Grave; Reach III has 2 Fertile, 1 Stony, 2 Memory Site, and 1 Grave.

## 6. The Loom of the Fates and Backlash

The Loom has three threads: Obsession, Acceptance, and Oblivion. Each card played face up or Burned adds one knot to its thread. A thread is full at 4 knots with 2–3 players, and at 5 in solo play or with 4 players.

When a thread becomes full, remove all its knots and resolve the **Backlash** for that thread:

- **Obsession, Grasping**: place a Shade token on the soul's space, left bank first: if the left bank already has a Shade token, place it on the right bank. If both banks already have one (or the soul is on the near shore), place it on the next space instead (again left bank first). If the bank it goes on has a Root or Bloom token, return that token to the supply first, then place the Shade token (section 7).
- **Acceptance, Resignation**: put the Resignation token next to the soul. In the next Soul Step the soul does not move and you forget the top card of the deck; then remove the token.
- **Oblivion, the Tide Rises**: place a Black Water token on the next space (section 7).

When the soul moves onto the first space of a new Reach, remove all knots from all three threads. The Echo stays where it is.

## 7. Black Water, Shades and Fractures

**Placing Black Water.** Black Water only ever goes on spaces ahead of the soul: never on the soul's own space or behind it. Put the Black Water token on the space named; if that space already has Black Water, use the first space after it (higher number) without Black Water; if every space after it has Black Water, use the space between it and the soul that is closest to it and free of Black Water. Then flip every Bloom token on that space to its Root side. The soul cannot move onto a space with Black Water. Black Water is always taken from the Ledger's Black Water well. If you must place Black Water and the well is empty, you lose (Deluge).

**Removing Black Water.** In Cleanup, if both banks of a space with Black Water have a Root or Bloom token, remove the Black Water token from the game. Cards that say "drain" remove Black Water the same way. Removed Black Water goes back in the box and never returns to the well.

**Placing a Shade.** Put the Shade token on the bank named. If that bank already has a Shade token, use the other bank of the same space; if both are taken, continue with the next space ahead. If the bank it goes on has a Root or Bloom token, return that token to the supply first, then place the Shade token. No Root can be placed on a bank with a Shade token. Shade tokens on both banks of the next space stop the soul, unless a player uses Push Through (section 3).

**Removing Shades.** In Cleanup, return a Shade token to the supply if the same bank of the space directly before it and the space directly after it both have a Bloom token. Cards that say "banish" or "wash away" also return Shade tokens to the supply. When a Shade token is removed, the bank tile under it keeps its own terrain (a Stony tile is still Stony, for example).

**Fractures** come from Shade damage, Push Through, Adrift and the harsh Mist card *Dark Wind*. Put each one on the next Fracture well of the Ledger; a mended Fracture comes off the highest well. At 3 Fractures you lose (Shattered). The card *Hold the Line* gives you the Shield token: it cancels the next Fracture and is then removed. If it is still unused at the end of its owner's next turn, remove it then.

## 8. Light and Mantles

**Gaining Light.** Light is a resource shared by all players and kept in a common Light pool. Each time the soul moves off a space, gain 1 Light for each Bloom token on that space (2 Light if both banks have one); the space is then cleared, so each Bloom gives Light only once. This happens whenever the soul moves: during the Soul Step, during an extra move, when a card moves the soul, or when a player uses Push Through. Light stays in the pool until it is spent or lost to the harsh Mist card *Dark Wind*. Also, with the *Open Crossing*, each Memory Site flipped gives 2 Light; with any other Soul, flipping a Memory Site gives no Light.

**Spending Light.** Light has two uses:

- **Recall** (as your Answer): pay 3 Light to move any card from the forgotten pile to the discard pile (section 3).
- **Summon** a Mantle (before your Answer): pay 3 Light (see below).

**Summoning a Mantle** (before your Answer): pay 3 Light (2 with the *King on the Barge*) and put one card from your hand that matches the Mantle's thread on the discard pile. Take the Mantle card. With 2–4 players, each player holds at most one Mantle and keeps it for the rest of the game. A Mantle cannot be swapped or given up, though the *King on the Barge* has a special rule that can take it away (section 10). In solo play you may replace your Mantle (section 11).

**The Spirit Warden's "once per round".** This means once between the start of the holder's turn and the start of their next turn; it also works on other players' turns. Example: Alex holds the Spirit Warden. At the end of Sam's turn, the soul would Linger, so the Warden triggers: the soul stays where it is, no card is forgotten, and Alex turns the Warden sideways. Alex straightens it at the start of Alex's next turn. When the Warden stops an Adrift move (only when the extra space is not Lit or has Black Water), the soul does not make the extra move and takes no Fracture.

## 9. The Book of Remembrance and the ending

The Book has one page per Reach: page I, *Who I Was* (Reach I); page II, *What I Did* (Reach II); page III, *What I Carry* (Reach III). Each page has four slots, **Place**, **Person**, **Deed**, and **Feeling**, which complete a sentence about the soul's life.

**Inscribing.** To inscribe a card is to place it permanently in the Book as part of the soul's story: instead of discarding it, slide it into its slot with the photo corners. It stays there for the rest of the game and is never drawn again. Inscribing is optional: when a card qualifies, you may inscribe it or discard it as usual. A card qualifies when:

- it Resonates and the slot shown on it is still empty on the page of the Reach the soul is in; or
- it is a **Key Memory** played face up, Resonating or not.
- Oblivion cards and Burned cards can never be inscribed.

**Page rewards.** Each page pays a small reward for each pair of neighboring slots: **Place + Person**, **Person + Deed**, and **Deed + Feeling**. When an inscription fills the second slot of a pair, resolve that pair's reward immediately. One card can complete two pairs (a Person inscribed next to a Place and a Deed): resolve both. Each reward happens only once per page; check off its box in the Book. The rewards are printed on each page:

| Page                   | Place + Person                                        | Person + Deed                  | Deed + Feeling                                            |
|------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| **I · Who I Was**      | Place 1 Root.                                         | Bloom 1 Root.                  | Gain 2 Light.                                             |
| **II · What I Did**    | Banish the nearest Shade.                             | Drain the nearest Black Water. | Untie 1 knot on the fullest thread (if tied, you choose). |
| **III · What I Carry** | Return the newest forgotten card to the discard pile. | Mend 1 Fracture.               | The soul steps forward now, if the next space is Lit.     |

**Chapter Blessing.** When all 4 slots of a page are filled (3 for the *Garland Girl*), the page also grants a Chapter Blessing immediately (once per page). You do not choose the Blessing: check these in order and resolve the first one that is possible:

1.  If the soul has at least one Fracture: remove 1 Fracture (from the highest well).
2.  Otherwise, if any space has Black Water: remove the nearest Black Water token ahead of the soul and return it to the box.
3.  Otherwise, if the forgotten pile has any cards: move the newest forgotten card to the discard pile.
4.  Otherwise nothing happens.

![book](figures/en_book.jpg)

### The ending

When the game ends, the Veiled Memories are lifted: everyone may reveal their cards and share the memories written on them. Then read the Book aloud, page by page, even after a loss. Read each page's slots in order, and read an empty slot as "…but this, the river has taken". Then read the keepsakes found beside that page: "…and from this Reach, all that was left: a torn banner, a dented helm."

**The ending card.** If you win, the soul's story ends with one of its ending cards (the *Open Crossing* has none). First check where the soul's **Key Memory** card is:

- **Inscribed in the Book**: the soul crosses remembering what mattered most.
- **In the forgotten pile**: the soul crosses without it, lighter and emptier.
- **Anywhere else** (the deck, a hand, or the discard pile): it was never spoken (the cards say "unspoken"), and the soul crosses with it unsaid.

Then, without reading the stories, look at the "Triggers when" line of each of the soul's ending cards and take the one that matches where the Key Memory is and how many cards were forgotten. A **hidden ending** whose conditions are all met replaces the ending it names. Read the chosen ending card aloud last.

If you lose, no ending card is read: the Book and its keepsakes are all that remain of the soul.

## 10. The Souls and the Lantern

*Four souls the old ferryman would not carry, and one with no name at all.*

Each named Soul has its own 8-card Memory pack, which includes one **Key Memory**, and its own special rule; the *Open Crossing* uses the 8 Echo cards instead. These cards are shuffled into the Memory deck in step 5 of the setup, and each Soul's special setup is listed in section 2. Each special rule below quotes the Soul card first, then explains it in game terms.

### Child of the Last Flame

A small child alone in a doorway on the coldest night of the year, with a handful of little lights to sell and none sold. To keep warm she lights them one by one, and in each flame she sees something: a warm stove, a feast, and at last the one person who ever held her, coming to take her home.

**Special rule:** "Starts with 3 Light. The first Fracture in each Reach is ignored. Never drifts Adrift. She can forget 1 more card before she Fades. No harsh Mist."

- **Starts with 3 Light**: put 3 Light in the Light pool during setup.
- **The first Fracture in each Reach is ignored**: the first time in each Reach the soul would take a Fracture, no Fracture is placed. Turn her Soul card sideways as a reminder, and straighten it when the soul moves onto the first space of a new Reach.
- **Never drifts Adrift**: with 9 or more forgotten, only the ***Light as Ash*** extra move (6 or more forgotten) applies: the soul moves one more space only if the next space is Lit and free of Black Water, and never takes a Fracture for it.
- **She can forget 1 more card before she Fades**: her Faded limit is 1 more than the Lantern's. At the Standard Lantern, for example, she Fades at 11 forgotten cards instead of 10.
- **No harsh Mist**: no harsh Mist cards go in the Mist decks.

**Key Memory:** *the one who used to hold me before she was gone*.

### The Garland Girl

A young woman who wove a flower for everyone she loved. Her father died, the man who promised to marry her turned cruel, and she gave her flowers away one by one and slipped into a brook still singing. She kept one flower for herself, and no one ever learned which.

**Special rule:** "Each page gives its Chapter Blessing at only 3 inscribed memories." That is, a page gives its Chapter Blessing as soon as 3 cards (instead of 4) are inscribed on it (section 9). **Key Memory:** *the one flower I kept for myself*.

### The King on the Barge

An old king who won every battle but the last. His closest friend loved his queen, his fellowship broke apart, and he fell on a field of his own making. Dying, he was laid on a black barge. The sword he was lent was never returned to the water it came from, and so he cannot rest.

**Special rule:** "Mantles cost 2 Light. A Fracture strips the Mantle of the player who caused it."

- **Mantles cost 2 Light**: summoning a Mantle costs 2 Light instead of 3 (section 8).
- **A Fracture strips the Mantle of the player who caused it**: when the soul takes a Fracture during a player's turn (including its end-of-turn steps), that player loses their Mantle; return it to the other Mantles, where it can be summoned again. A Fracture canceled by the Shield token does not count as taken.

**Key Memory:** *the hand that rose from the lake to give me a sword*.

### The Bride Who Stayed Below

She died on her wedding day. Her husband came down into the dark to bring her back, on one condition: he must not look back until they reached the light. One step from the surface, he turned. She has stood in the shadows ever since, deciding whether to remember his face or let it go.

**Special rule:** "Starts with 1 Light. The first time she would Linger in the dark, she is pulled one space back instead of forgetting."

- **Starts with 1 Light**: put 1 Light in the Light pool during setup.
- **Her hidden ending**: one of her ending cards needs a game in which the soul never Lingers. The first time the soul Lingers, turn her Soul card upside down as a reminder.
- **Pulled one space back** (once per game): in the Soul Step, when the soul Lingers and would forget the top card of the deck (her space is not Lit, and neither the Hold Fast token nor the Spirit Warden stops it; section 4, step d, IV), move the soul token back one space (to the space numbered 1 lower) instead. Nothing is forgotten and no Light is gained. Then turn her Soul card sideways to show it has been used. It does not apply while the soul is on the near shore (space 0).

**Key Memory:** *his face, the instant he turned to look at me*.

### Open Crossing

A nameless soul. You do not learn its story; you write it.

**Special rule:** "Memory Sites give +2 Light." That is, each Memory Site flipped gives 2 Light; this Soul has no Keepsake cards (section 5). There is no Key Memory and no ending card; the Book is the soul's whole story.

![souls](figures/en_souls.jpg)

### The Lantern

The Lantern sets the difficulty and is chosen in step 4 of the setup (section 2). **Standard** is the baseline: an experienced group should win about half of its games. Use **Gentle** to learn the game, and **Hard** or **Heroic** for a greater challenge.

| Lantern       | Black Water supply | Faded at     | Harsh Mist per Reach |
|---------------|--------------------|--------------|----------------------|
| ◆◇◇◇ Gentle   | 6                  | 11 forgotten | 0                    |
| ◆◆◇◇ Standard | 5                  | 10 forgotten | 2                    |
| ◆◆◆◇ Hard     | 4                  | 9 forgotten  | 3                    |

## 11. Solo play and the Ghost

In solo play you hold 4 cards and each Loom thread is full at 5 knots.

**The Ghost.** After each of your turns (end-of-turn step 6), reveal the top card of the Ghost deck and resolve its action, following the same placement rules as a player (section 5). Then add a knot to its thread (a full thread triggers its Backlash) and move the Echo there, so your next Answer can Resonate with it. When the Ghost deck is empty, shuffle all Ghost cards into a new deck.

**Replacing a Mantle.** You may summon a new Mantle even while you hold one. Flip the old Mantle face down; it can never be summoned again. Each Mantle can be summoned only once, and you may summon at most **3** times per game.

| Obsession  | 2   | Roots on the nearest open bank                    |
| Acceptance | 2   | Blooms the nearest Root                           |
| Acceptance | 2   | Blooms the furthest Root                          |
| Oblivion   | 2   | Banishes the nearest Shade, or drains Black Water |
